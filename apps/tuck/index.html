Tuck

A tidier menu bar, one click away.

A tiny Mac app that hides the menu bar icons you don’t need. Tick them once, then click the arrow to tuck them away.

Download for Mac

v1.0 · 1.3 MB ZIP · Free
Apple silicon (M1 or newer) · macOS 27+

What’s inside

One click
Click the arrow or press ⌘⇧2 to hide or show your icons. They tuck away again by themselves after 10 seconds, but never while you’re using a menu.
Pick from a list
Right-click the arrow and tick the apps you want hidden. Tick as many as you like in one go. No dragging icons around.
Running apps at a glance
See every open app with the memory it’s using, helpers included. Spot apps hidden with ⌘H or running without a Dock icon, and show or quit any of them.
Starts with your Mac
Launch at Login is on from the first run. There’s no settings window to visit.
Light by design
About 13 MB of memory and no background polling. Nothing runs until you click.
Fails safe
If anything goes wrong, your icons simply stay visible. Quit Tuck and every icon comes back instantly.

Getting started

  1. Download and unzip Tuck. Drag Tuck.app into Applications (it only works from there), then open it.
  2. Grant it Accessibility access in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Tuck uses it to see which apps have menu bar icons.
  3. Right-click Tuck’s arrow, tick the apps to hide, then click the arrow.

This is a personal build and isn’t notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks it and you trust this download, use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway after trying to open it.

Quit Hidden Bar or any other menu bar manager first: only one app can control the menu bar at a time.

While icons are tucked, macOS ignores clicks on the clock. Tuck notices the click, brings your icons back, and opens Notification Center for you.

The rabbit hole

After Peel, I pointed REA at Hidden Bar, the open-source menu bar app I’d used for years. On macOS 27 it had become fiddly for me: a Dock icon popping up, menus closing mid-click, and more settings than I ever touched.

Tuck is the version I wanted: one arrow, one list, nothing running in the background. It uses the same macOS 27 mechanism Hidden Bar does, and reuses Hidden Bar’s small MIT-licensed bridge to it, with thanks to Dwarves Foundation and Console Labs. There’s no affiliation.

A few rough edges

Tuck needs macOS 27 and relies on a private macOS feature, so a future update could stop it from hiding. If that happens, your icons simply stay visible. While icons are tucked, macOS also hides the label naming the app using your microphone or camera; the coloured dot in the corner of the screen still shows.

Apple’s own icons (clock, Wi-Fi, battery, Control Center) can’t be hidden, and an app with several icons hides them all together. This build has been checked on my Mac; broader compatibility is still a work in progress.
